# Budget and flighting

Educational summary written for the Campaign Doctor demo. It is not vendor documentation.

## Flight dates and budget

A flight is the date range a campaign runs, and the budget is the total it may spend across that flight. Expected spend at any point is the budget spread evenly over the flight days, which is the baseline for the pacing ratio. Flight dates are inclusive, so a flight from 1 October to 31 October is 31 days.

## Daily caps

A daily cap limits spend per day regardless of the total budget. Daily caps protect against a single day burning a large share of the budget, but a cap set too low guarantees under-pacing, and a cap set too high does little to prevent front-loading.

## Budget exhaustion

When spend reaches the budget the campaign stops delivering. If this happens before the flight ends, the campaign was over-pacing earlier. Check the days remaining against the budget remaining: a campaign that has spent most of its budget with many days left needs a lower bid, even pacing, or more budget.

## Changing settings mid-flight

Raising a bid or switching to accelerated pacing changes spend within hours, while audience and creative changes can take longer to show. After any change, watch the daily metrics for two to three days before judging the effect, and avoid changing several settings at once, because it hides which change worked.
