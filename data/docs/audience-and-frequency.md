# Audience and frequency

Educational summary written for the Campaign Doctor demo. It is not vendor documentation.

## Audience size and bid volume

The audience is the set of users a campaign is allowed to reach. A larger audience gives more matching auctions and so more bids. A small audience, such as a retargeting pool of recent site visitors, produces few bid opportunities no matter how high the bid is. A campaign with a small audience and a high win rate but low spend is limited by reach, not price.

## Frequency caps

A frequency cap limits how many times one user can see the ad in a period, for example three impressions per day. Caps protect users from over-exposure and avoid wasted spend. They also set a ceiling on delivery: the most impressions a day is roughly the number of reachable users times the cap. With a small audience and a low cap, that ceiling can sit below what the budget needs.

## Retargeting pools

Retargeting audiences are small and shrink as users age out of the lookback window. They convert well and win auctions easily, because bids for known users are high, but they cannot absorb a large budget. Pair retargeting with a prospecting campaign if the budget is larger than the pool can use.

## Widening an audience

To lift bid volume, widen targeting in steps: extend lookback windows, add lookalike or similar audiences, loosen geography or device filters, and relax contextual or brand-safety filters that are stricter than the brief requires. Change one filter at a time so you can see which one added volume.
