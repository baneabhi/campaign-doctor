# Bidding and win rate

Educational summary written for the Campaign Doctor demo. It is not vendor documentation.

## Win rate

Win rate is wins divided by bids: the share of submitted bids that won the auction. It is a diagnostic, not a goal. A low win rate usually means bids are below the price needed to win, so the campaign cannot buy enough inventory. A very high win rate, often above 60 percent, can mean the campaign is bidding more than it needs to.

## Clearing price and effective CPM

The clearing price is the price at which an impression is actually sold. Effective CPM is spend divided by impressions, multiplied by 1000, and shows what the campaign really pays per thousand impressions. In a first-price auction the winner pays its own bid, so effective CPM tracks bid levels closely. In a second-price auction the winner pays just above the runner-up. Many exchanges now run first-price auctions, and bidders often use bid shading to avoid overpaying.

## Max bid

The max bid CPM is the ceiling the campaign may bid. When the max bid sits at or below typical clearing prices for the targeted inventory, win rate falls and delivery stalls. Raising the max bid raises win rate and effective CPM, so it buys delivery at a higher unit price. Compare effective CPM with the max bid: if effective CPM is close to the max bid, the cap is probably binding.

## Bid volume

Bid volume is how many auctions the campaign enters. It depends on how much inventory matches the targeting. A campaign with plenty of bids but a low win rate has a price problem. A campaign with few bids and a healthy win rate has a reach or targeting problem. Raising the bid fixes the first and does nothing for the second.
